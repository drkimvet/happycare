## NAC: three families, do not mix

Published schedules are **indication-specific**. Instinct will retrieve Plumb. You will not invent a blend.

1. **Acetaminophen** — public ASPCA / VECCS historical protocol family: load-then-maintenance NAC (classic published load 140 mg/kg, severe published load 280 mg/kg, then 70 mg/kg). Charcoal can adsorb oral NAC; separate them. △ confirm current Plumb/hospital numbers and route.
2. **Xylitol** — Merck: dextrose is the treatment that is actually evidenced; NAC is "consider," efficacy for the liver injury is unknown. If the hospital uses a xylitol NAC schedule, it is **not** the APAP schedule. △ Plumb.
3. **Established hepatic failure / cycad** — a third published family (often 140 then 70 in older liver protocols). Do not borrow the xylitol interval or the APAP 280 load unless that is what Plumb lists for **this** indication.

If you cannot name which family you are in, you are not writing NAC.

## Fluids, AKI, abdomen

- AAHA fluid therapy 2024: shock bolus ≠ "maintenance drip all night." Reassess perfusion, electrolytes, and UOP. Do not run a number because it is round.
- Name the shock type before you bolus: hypovolemic vs cardiogenic vs distributive vs obstructive.
- Cardiogenic / CHF: fluids are often avoided (preload). AAHA: hypotension in CHF is an **inotrope** conversation, not a reflexive shock bolus. Furosemide is the edema conversation; △ Plumb. Do not harvest 2013 furosemide or hypertonic-saline tables.
- Hypovolemic: bolus ≠ overnight drip. Cats often show **bradycardia, hypothermia, hypotension** — do not wait for a dog-style fast heart rate.
- Never bolus a bag that contains KCl (AAHA).
- GDV: obstructive + hypovolemic. Right lateral radiograph. Do not induce emesis. Do not watch overnight. Do not harvest 2013 orogastric/trocar recipes. Do not invent a lactate cutoff — serial change after fluids matters more than one number (Merck associates high lactate with necrosis risk; it is not a euthanasia line). Gastropexy prevents volvulus recurrence, not all bloat. Post-op VPCs are common and delayed; △ lidocaine. Never bolus a KCl bag.
- Sepsis / septic shock is **distributive** (± hypovolemic ± cardiogenic). Infection plus organ dysfunction, not a SIRS checkbox (2025 consensus). Find the pocket: abdomen, uterus, urine, chest, bite, catheter, GI leak. Source control is the surgery conversation. Culture if it does not delay the first antimicrobial (Merck: waiting decreases survival). Cats can be **hypothermic and bradycardic** — do not wait for dog-style fever + tachycardia. High-dose steroids are not recommended in septic shock (Merck triage). Azotemic cat: still no DexSP. Addison is a different syringe. Do not invent SIRS HR/RR/temp/WBC bands or a lactate/MAP as diagnosis or euthanasia. Do not harvest 2013 shock-dose / hetastarch tables. Never bolus a KCl bag. Antibiotics do not replace OHE, a septic-abdomen explore, or a chest tube.
- Cat respiratory distress: **name the space before the syringe.** Oxygen, minimal handling (Merck). Inspiratory stertor/stridor = upper airway. Expiratory push / wheeze = bronchial (asthma/bronchitis) until TFAST says otherwise. Quiet restrictive chest = pleural (tap first — already gated). B-lines + big LA / gallop = CHF Lasix conversation, △ Plumb. Pale = anemia. Cold pulseless legs = FATE. Cat + vaccine/sting = anaphylaxis (respiratory shock organ). Do not stack Lasix + albuterol + DexSP. Do not wrestle for radiographs. Do not harvest Merck/TVP puff or terbutaline/DexSP mg/kg lines. New cough in an older cat is often pneumonia, not new asthma (Merck owner). Azotemic cat: still no DexSP. Bronchodilator is rescue, not chronic monotherapy.
- Dog hemorrhagic diarrhea: **not a diagnosis.** AHDS (old “HGE”): acute vomit + bloody diarrhea, often hemoconcentrated; **IV fluids are the mainstay** (Merck). Parenteral antibiotics are **not routine** in mild–moderate AHDS; they are a sepsis / neutropenia / failing-perfusion conversation. Young or under-vaccinated: fecal parvo antigen **even if the diarrhea is not bloody** (Merck: ~25% of parvo). Neutropenia pushes parvo/sepsis, not “just AHDS.” Isolate parvo. DDX still includes Addison, anticoagulant rodenticide, FB/intussusception, ulcer, pancreatitis. Do not invent a PCV or neutrophil cutoff. Do not harvest ampicillin or 2013 fluid tables. Never bolus a KCl bag. Offer food when vomiting is controlled (parvo needs nutrition). Same household: do not copy the bloody-diarrhea discharge onto a well housemate, and do not skip isolation if THIS dog is parvo.
- Eclampsia / puerperal tetany: nursing (or periparturient) small-breed bitch, peak lactation 2–3 weeks, large litter — also any breed, any time in lactation, rarely late gestation; uncommon in queens (Merck). Tremor, stiffness, panting, then seizures. Pull ionized/total calcium but **do not wait for the printer** on the classic picture. Slow IV **calcium gluconate** with ECG; stop or slow for bradycardia. **Calcium chloride is not for SQ.** Glucose now (can coexist). Interrupt nursing tonight; milk-replacer conversation. **Oral calcium during pregnancy predisposes**, it does not prevent (Merck). Do not harvest mL/kg or mg/dL cutoffs. Do not treat as idiopathic epilepsy. Tetany fever is not a default ice-water heatstroke protocol. △ Plumb.
- Dystocia: **obstruction vs inertia before oxytocin.** Merck: **oxytocin is not for a stuck fetus** (can separate the placenta). Dark green or malodorous discharge **before** the first puppy or kitten is placental separation — come now, do not invent an hour table (sources disagree). Glucose and calcium if inertia is the story (links to eclampsia). Two Merck pages disagree on SQ calcium gluconate; night crisis is slow IV with ECG, not CaCl SQ. Brachycephalic / old pelvic fracture / singleton oversized / fetal distress / failed medical → C-section conversation tonight. Do not send oxytocin home with the breeder. Do not harvest IU, “three doses,” or fetal-HR cutoffs. △ Plumb / hospital.
- Toy puppy hypoglycemia: **glucose now is the syringe**, not the diagnosis (Merck: toy-breed puppy hypoglycemia in the first 6 months; frequent commercial puppy meals; usually resolves with maturity). Warm them. If they can swallow, feed. If collapsed, **do not pour syrup into a collapsed mouth** — rub a small amount on the gums or go IV/IO (aspiration). Printed 25%/50% mL/kg, 60 mg/dL, and neonate 10% tables stay on the page — △ Plumb. Perivascular 50% dextrose is a phlebitis/slough conversation: dilute, catheter. Sepsis, PSS, parvo, parasites, missed meals, xylitol stay on the DDX. An 8-week Yorkie is **not an insulinoma puppy**. Do not NPO. Do not send home until they eat and hold glucose. ## Feline pancreatitis / tense cranial abdomen (Forman ACVIM 2021)

- Localize first. Tense or painful **upper / cranial** abdomen is pancreas, stomach, biliary tree, or cranial SI (linear FB still on the list). Days of tension are not tonight's meal until the timeline is written down.
- Diagnosis is the **cluster**: signs + imaging + fPLI. SNAP fPL normal makes pancreatitis less likely; abnormal includes the equivocal range. **Weak positive SNAP is abnormal, not a diagnosis and not a negative.** Spec fPL supports; it does **not** rule pancreatitis in or out by itself. Do not invent the lab cutoff; the report names its own reference. Do not copy this SNAP onto a housemate.
- Do not withhold food "to rest the pancreas." Forman: any further fasting is not recommended; hepatic lipidosis is the cat-specific cost. Offer PO early or a feeding-tube conversation.
- Do not copy a canine **low-fat 3–5 day** prescription as pancreatitis therapy. Forman: evidence that dietary fat is deleterious in cats is not available. 3–5 days is the allium hemolysis watch, not a diet duration.
- Hard/dry beef jerky is a **digestibility** problem (dry, salt, connective tissue). Soft, highly digestible small meals are reasonable. Low fat is fine as that lever (not greasy leftovers). **High fiber is not easy-to-digest** — it adds residue/bulk (hairball/weight/constipation diets). Offer food if obstruction is off the table. No allium snacks.
- Support is fluids (AAHA: reassess perfusion, not a round rate), antiemetic, and **opioid** analgesia. Forman: opioids are primary analgesics in feline acute pancreatitis; **buprenorphine is adequate for most cats** (methadone/fentanyl if more severe). A one-time in-hospital dose is analgesia, not a disease-modifier and not a reason to NPO. Do not withhold it for theoretical sphincter-of-Oddi spasm. Ileus is a watch if linear FB is still on the list, not a reason to leave pain untreated. Do not switch to an NSAID because the SNAP was weak. **Gabapentin does not replace the opioid** for an overt/acute bout — Forman lists it as a PO option and as long-term chronic adjunct (with tramadol). Both sentences can be true: buprenorphine tonight, gabapentin later if it is chronic. △ Plumb. Read the gabapentin bottle (some human liquids have xylitol). Do not copy it onto a housemate. **A cc/mL draw is not a dose** until THIS vial's mg/mL and the weight are on the note (0.3 mg/mL buprenorphine ≠ Simbadol 1.8 mg/mL ≠ hydromorphone). Chart mg, then △ Plumb. Antibiotics are not routine for uncomplicated sterile pancreatitis. DexSP / NSAID is not the pancreatitis plan.
- Sucralfate is a GI coating (ulcer / esophagitis conversation), not pancreatitis therapy. Separate it from other orals. △ Plumb for sucralfate, fluids, and the opioid.
- A fatty or seasoned snack can sit on top of days of cranial pain. If allium (onion/garlic powder) is also on the list, the Heinz-body clock is still open. Do not clear on tonight's PCV.

- Same snack is not the same disease. Cranial-abdomen / pain cat: Forman cluster, opioid conversation, `dc-gi` if that is THIS cat's localization. Exposure-only sibling: allium Heinz clock, `dc-toxin`. Do not copy the discharge.

- Do not NPO a vomiting cat for **12 hours**. Forman: hepatic lipidosis. If vomiting continues, call — do not paste a canine bland-diet fast.
- Do not paste leftover dentistry or heart paragraphs onto a toxin sheet.

- NSAID: hold on azotemia, hypovolemia, or unknown kidney function. On-label NSAIDs only when you are actually reaching for one; △ Plumb.
- DexSP: not a treatment for feline AKI. Not a substitute for localization.
- Cefazolin CRI: not a default because the cat is sick. Perioperative cefazolin extra-label use is a Gonzalez 2017 conversation, not a night CRI.
- Azodyl / "gut-renal" nutraceuticals: do not add them to an anorexic AKI cat as if they decompress a kidney.
- Aluminum hydroxide: hold if the patient is not eating.
- Opioid: pain is part of obstructive/AKI care. Name the drug, then △ Plumb for the dose. Do not leave a painful hydro/pyonephrosis cat on "no analgesic" because creatinine is high.

## Rodenticide (four families, not one vitamin K)

1. Anticoagulant → vitamin K1 pathway, after you have the right product.
2. Bromethalin → neuro; not vitamin K.
3. Cholecalciferol → hypercalcemia/AKI; not vitamin K.
4. Zinc phosphide → PH3; not vitamin K.

Unknown block: treat as unknown, not as "warfarin."

## Addisonian crisis (dog; Merck Jul 2024)

- Great imitator. Waxing-waning GI, collapse, **relative bradycardia in shock**, hyperK, hypoNa, prerenal azotemia that looks like AKI or a blocked dog.
- Na:K <27 is a hint, not a diagnosis. Atypical Addison can have **normal electrolytes**.
- Fluids first. Current conversation is a **balanced crystalloid**, reassess perfusion. Do not copy the 2013 0.9% NaCl hourly recipe. If Na is very low, do not jack it (myelinolysis).
- Treat the ECG, not the K number. Calcium gluconate is cardioprotection. Insulin+dextrose only after glucose is known.
- Confirm with ACTH stim. Baseline cortisol >2 µg/dL rules it out; a low baseline does not rule it in. The 2013 book printing **mg/dL** for that cutoff is a unit trap.
- Prednisolone/hydrocortisone **cross-react** on the cortisol assay. Merck: DexSP can go before the draw if the dog cannot wait. △ every steroid/DOCP number in Plumb.
- Do not send home as gastroenteritis. Cats are rare.

## DKA / HHS (dog and cat; Merck + AAHA 2026 feline DKA)

- Fluids first. Insulin after perfusion is moving and potassium is not already low. Do not copy the 2013 40–60 mL/kg/h recipe.
- Find the trigger: UTI, pancreatitis, steroids, SGLT2 if a cat is on one.
- Urine dipstick misses β-hydroxybutyrate. A negative strip does not rule out DKA.
- Do not give bicarbonate as the default for the gap acidosis. Fluids + insulin close most of it.
- Do not drop glucose so fast you risk cerebral edema. Goal is to stop ketosis, not euglycemia tonight.
- HHS is the other decompensated diabetic: very high glucose/osmolality, little/no ketones. Do not treat it as DKA and do not dump hypotonic fluid into a chronic hypernatremia.
- △ every insulin CRI and K-supplement number in Plumb or the hospital protocol.

## Seizure emergency (dog and cat; ACVIM 2024 SE/CS + Merck)

- Status epilepticus is **>5 minutes** or two or more seizures without recovery — not the 2013 “30-minute” line. Cluster is ≥2 in 24 hours.
- Glucose, temperature, and a toxin story first (reactive vs epileptic). Do not call syncope a seizure.
- First line is a benzodiazepine. ACVIM: midazolam IV or **intranasal** is the current conversation if there is no vein. △ the crash-cart / Plumb number.
- Then load a maintenance drug (phenobarbital and/or levetiracetam). Do not harvest the 2013 pentobarbital-second-line table.
- Do not send a **cat** home on **oral diazepam** (idiosyncratic hepatic necrosis). Oral diazepam is not dog maintenance either (Merck).
- Book phenobarbital “20–40 g/mL” is a unit trap (µg/mL). △ the lab.
- Propofol / anesthesia for refractory SE only with an airway plan. Do not invent a CRI.

## UTI (ISCAID 2019, not a 2011 leftover)

- Subclinical bacteriuria is not a UTI and is rarely treated.
- Sporadic bacterial cystitis is 3–5 days, not a 14-day habit.
- First-tier conversation: amoxicillin (± clavulanate if that is what is on the shelf) or TMS. Reserve FQ and 3rd-gen cephalosporins.
- Young cats with FLUTD signs: empiric antibiotic is rarely indicated. Analgesia first.
- **Confirmed UTI is infection, not FIC.** Still 3–5 days if it is sporadic lower tract. Fever, lumbar pain, or azotemia flips to pyelo.
- **UTI does not close hypercalcemia.** Two problem lists. Image for CaOx.
- Do not treat a urinary catheter because it is there.
- Pyelonephritis is a tissue infection (different plan/duration). A positive culture after clinical cure may just be bacteriuria again.
- △ every mg/kg in Plumb. NSAID for dysuria: hold if azotemic.

## CPA (Pardo / RECOVER 2024)

- C-A-B. 100–120 compressions/min. 2-minute cycles. Ventilate the intubated patient without stopping compressions.
- High-dose epinephrine is out. Standard-dose only, from the crash-cart chart.
- Atropine, if used: once and early, when high vagal tone is the story. Do not stack it.
- Bag-mask before mouth-to-snout.
- Dogs and cats only. Do not copy this cart onto a horse or a cow.
- △ the cart sticker / Plumb. Do not generate a mg/kg here.

## Consent sentence when the disease is decompressedable

If imaging or palpation says one kidney is fluid-filled / non-functional and the other is compromised, the owner conversation is **referral, decompression, or euthanasia**, not "we will watch the belly overnight." Write that in the plan.
