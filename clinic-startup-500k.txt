Starting a clinic with about $500,000 — Dr. Yujin Kim, NY
Not legal or tax advice. Talk to a NY veterinary practice attorney and a CPA
before you sign a lease or a purchase agreement.

$500,000 is enough to get off other people’s payroll. It is not enough for a
Midtown build-from-scratch hospital. Do not spend it proving a point to URvet.
Send the $5,950 demand, then stop taking their shifts.

------------------------------------------------
What $500k actually buys in 2026
------------------------------------------------
De novo (new) small-animal GP, US average: $300k–$1M all-in.
NY metro runs high. Leasehold build-out often $150k–$400k. Equipment
$80k–$300k. Six months of payroll/rent $100k–$250k. Ground-up
construction $275–$400 / sq ft before soft costs — skip that.

Buy an existing GP: many sell $400k–$1.5M (often ~0.65–0.85× trailing
gross, or an EBITDA multiple). SBA 7(a) can do ~10% down plus a seller
note. Your $500k is the down payment + closing + six months of oxygen,
not the whole purchase price of a busy 3-doctor hospital.

Mobile / house-call: $50k–$150k to start. Rest stays in the bank.

------------------------------------------------
Pick one path (do not mix them)
------------------------------------------------
A) BUY a small existing practice on Long Island / outer boroughs  [best]
   Immediate revenue, staff, DEA cabinet, clients. You are the boss on
   day one. Use a vet-practice broker + SBA lender + your own CPA.
   Walk from anything that needs you to drain the $500k to zero.

B) LEASE a former clinic (already plumbed for wet tables / x-ray)
   Two exam rooms. Used digital rad + dental. One LVT + one CSR.
   Nassau / western Suffolk / Queens — not 57th Street.

C) HOUSE-CALL or mobile first
   Fastest exit from URvet. Keep LIU + GLG. Add scheduled home visits
   and vaccine/wellness. Open a room later with cash flow, not hope.

D) Do not do
   Midtown Manhattan de novo. Ground-up construction. Buying a dying
   ER that needs $1M of equipment. A partner who “knows business”
   and wants your cash.

------------------------------------------------
Week 1–2 — entity and money
------------------------------------------------
1. NY professional entity: typically PLLC or PC (vets cannot just use a
   regular LLC for the professional practice). NY attorney who does
   professional entities. EIN. Dedicated business bank. Do not commingle.
2. Park the $500k. Do not buy an x-ray this week.
3. CPA: tax year, sales tax, payroll service, 1099 vs W-2 for staff.
4. Personal runway separate from clinic cash: 6 months of YOUR bills
   (LIU pay already helps). Clinic working capital is extra.

------------------------------------------------
Week 2–6 — NY licenses (start these while you shop)
------------------------------------------------
You already have DVM, PhD. Confirm:
  • NY veterinarian license is current and registered
    https://www.op.nysed.gov/professions/veterinarian
  • DEA registration in YOUR name at the new address (cannot prescribe
    controlled drugs without it)
  • If the hospital will stock/dispense as an institution: NYS DOH
    Class 3 controlled-substance license, then DEA for the facility
  • NYS Board of Pharmacy rules if you run an in-house pharmacy
  • USDA accreditation (if you do health certificates)
  • Town/village CO, veterinary occupancy, medical waste, radiation
    (x-ray) permit, sharps, DEA safe bolted down
  • Malpractice (PLIT or equivalent), general liability, property,
    workers’ comp, cyber (records)
  • OSHA, SDS, radiation badges

NY does not hand you a single “clinic license.” The building, the
drugs, and your personal license are separate.

------------------------------------------------
Week 2–8 — find the practice or the box
------------------------------------------------
Buy path
  • Brokers: vet-specific (not a random commercial realtor)
  • NDA, then trailing 24 months P&L, production by doctor, rent,
    staff list, equipment list, non-competes
  • Independent valuation. Do not trust the seller’s multiple.
  • Red flags: owner is the only doctor and is leaving town; rent resets
    in 12 months; no dental; AR older than 90 days; “cash” not on books

Lease path
  • Only spaces that were already a vet, dentist, or medical with drains
  • Landlord must allow veterinary use, boarding if you want it, and
    after-hours
  • 5–10 year lease, TI allowance, no personal guarantee if you can
    avoid it (you often cannot — cap it)

------------------------------------------------
What to open with (lean GP)
------------------------------------------------
Must have day one
  Two exam rooms, surgery + wet table, anesthesia, monitoring,
  dental unit + digital dental, digital rad (used is fine),
  in-house or reference lab, pharmacy starter, practice-management
  software, phones, payment processing, website + Google Business

Lease or wait
  Ultrasound, endoscope, chemotherapy, CT

Staff day one
  You + 1 LVT + 1 CSR. Do not hire a hospital manager in month one.

If the split does not fit, you are in the wrong space. Go mobile
or buy with SBA instead of forcing a pretty build.
